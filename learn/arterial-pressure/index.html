What the two blood pressure numbers measure

Where the average comes from, and what the gap between the numbers says.

Lesson 3 of 10

A blood pressure reading gives two numbers. The top one is the peak reached while the heart is ejecting. The bottom one is how far the pressure has fallen by the end of the pause, a moment before the next beat arrives.

Pressure is the push. Flow is what actually reaches an organ. They usually travel together, which is what makes the reading useful, and they come apart as soon as the vessels downstream have changed, which is why a normal reading is no promise that anything is being supplied.
